**The argument.** Many of the things students and teachers do to learn, such as rereading and cramming, feel productive and do little. Brown, Roediger and McDaniel explain the research behind methods that work better, and many of them feel harder while you do them. The book is written for the general reader, with stories and research side by side.

## Ideas worth keeping

- **Retrieval practice.** Trying to recall something strengthens memory more than reading it again.
- **Spacing.** Revisiting material after a gap works better than doing it all at once.
- **Interleaving.** Mixing different kinds of problems builds the skill of choosing the method.
- **Desirable difficulties.** Effort that feels slower can produce learning that lasts.
- **Feeling of knowing is unreliable.** Fluency while rereading is not the same as learning.

## Where it shows in Classroom Manager

Short, frequent, low-stakes quizzes are a natural fit for classroom computers. Classroom Manager can send a quiz file to every computer and collect the answers, and its exam mode applies classroom restrictions during a test. What to ask, and when, is the teacher's decision.

## Who should read it

Teachers, students at any level, and parents who want to understand how learning works.
