**The argument.** Ries argues that a startup is a human institution designed to deliver a new product under conditions of extreme uncertainty, and that the main job is to find out what customers really want before running out of money. His method is a loop of building, measuring and learning, using the smallest experiment that can teach you something.

## Ideas worth keeping

- **Build, measure, learn.** Treat every release as an experiment.
- **Minimum viable product.** The smallest version that can teach you something.
- **Validated learning.** Progress is what you have proven about customers, not what you have built.
- **Pivot or persevere.** Decide with evidence whether to change direction.

## Where it shows in Classroom Manager

Classroom Manager is built in small steps and tested on real computers, and the free Host lets a teacher try the program without an account before a school commits to anything. Each step is a small experiment.

## Who should read it

Anyone thinking about starting a software company, and teachers or students who want to understand how new products begin.
