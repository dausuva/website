**The argument.** Most teachers plan by choosing activities and hoping understanding follows. Wiggins and McTighe argue for planning backward from the goal. First decide what students should understand and be able to do. Then decide what evidence would show it. Only then plan the lessons. The expanded second edition adds templates and examples.

## Ideas worth keeping

- **Backward design.** Start with the desired results, then the evidence, then the learning plan.
- **Understanding is more than knowing.** Students should be able to explain, apply and transfer ideas.
- **Big ideas and essential questions** give a unit its spine.
- **Assessment is planned early**, not added at the end.
- **Watch for two traps:** activities for their own sake, and coverage for its own sake.

## Where it shows in Classroom Manager

Decide what a lesson is for before choosing the technology. A tool such as Classroom Manager is useful when it serves a clear goal, such as collecting work for review, and not when it is used because it is available.

## Who should read it

Teachers planning units and courses, heads of department, and anyone designing training or curriculum.
