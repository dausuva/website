**The argument.** Lemov studied teachers whose students did unusually well and wrote down, in plain language, what those teachers actually did. The result is a set of named, learnable techniques rather than a theory. The third edition revises the whole book, adds an opening chapter on mental models for decisions in the classroom, a new chapter on lesson preparation and ten new techniques.

## Ideas worth keeping

- **Named techniques.** Each one has a name, so a teacher or a department can talk about it and practise it.
- **Check for understanding.** Find out what every student knows during the lesson, not after the test.
- **Participation.** Techniques such as Cold Call and No Opt Out raise how many students think in every lesson.
- **Routines and procedures.** Time is saved when the start of a lesson is predictable.
- **Practice.** The craft of teaching improves through deliberate rehearsal, like any other.

## Where it shows in Classroom Manager

Classroom Manager exists to take routine computer tasks, such as sending or collecting files, off a teacher's mind, so that attention stays on the students. The same thought runs through this book: remove friction from the routine and the lesson is better.

## Who should read it

Teachers at any stage, school leaders who coach teachers, and student teachers looking for concrete practice.
