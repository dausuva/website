**The argument.** Petzold explains how computers work by building one on paper. He begins with simple codes such as Morse code and Braille, moves to switches, relays and logic gates, and arrives, chapter by chapter, at memory, a processor and software. By the end the reader understands what a computer is doing underneath, with no step skipped. The second edition adds new chapters and goes deeper into how the CPU is built.

## Ideas worth keeping

- **Codes carry information.** Everything a computer stores is a pattern of simple signals.
- **Logic gates** turn those signals into decisions and arithmetic.
- **Memory and the processor** appear as natural next steps, not as magic.
- **Software** is the last layer, sitting on everything below it.

## Where it shows in Classroom Manager

Everything Classroom Manager does, from sending a command to locking a screen, rests on these layers. Knowing what is underneath makes it easier to find bugs and to judge claims about what software can and cannot do.

## Who should read it

Curious readers, IB Computer Science students who want to understand hardware, and developers who learned to program without learning how the machine works.
