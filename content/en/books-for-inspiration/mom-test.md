**The argument.** People who love you will tell you your idea is great, even when it is not. Fitzpatrick offers a short set of rules for asking questions that even your mother could not answer with a lie: talk about their life instead of your idea, ask about specifics in the past instead of opinions about the future, and listen more than you talk.

## Ideas worth keeping

- **Talk about their life**, not your idea.
- **Ask about the past**, such as what they did last time, not what they might do.
- **Compliments are noise.** Look for commitments and facts instead.
- **Find out how they solve the problem today**, and what it costs them.

## Where it shows in Classroom Manager

Asking a teacher how a lesson runs today, before suggesting any feature, is the habit this book teaches. It is a short book that anyone can apply the next day.

## Who should read it

Founders, product managers, and students planning a project that other people will use.
