**The argument.** Willingham, a cognitive psychologist, takes questions teachers ask, such as why students remember stories but forget facts, and answers them using research on how the mind works. Each chapter ends with what the finding means for teaching. The second edition updates the research since the first.

## Ideas worth keeping

- **Thinking is hard and slow**, and the mind avoids it unless the task is right.
- **Factual knowledge matters.** Skills like reading and critical thinking depend on what you already know.
- **Working memory is small.** Lessons must not overload it.
- **Practice is needed** for any skill to become fluent.
- **Everyone thinks differently, but not in the ways often claimed.** Be careful with labels such as learning styles.

## Where it shows in Classroom Manager

The lesson is to keep the load on students' minds on the subject, not on the tools. A teacher who is not fighting the computers has more attention for the learners, which is why classroom software should disappear into the background.

## Who should read it

Teachers who want to ground their practice in research, and parents and school leaders who want a clear, short introduction to the science of learning.
