**The argument.** The authors review research on classroom management and turn it into practical steps for teachers. The book covers setting up rules and procedures, using disciplinary interventions appropriately, and building positive relationships with students, with the idea that management is a set of skills that can be learned.

## Ideas worth keeping

- **Rules and procedures** that students understand and help to shape.
- **Disciplinary interventions** that are proportionate and consistent.
- **Teacher-student relationships** as the base of good management.
- **A calm, alert presence** that notices what is happening across the room.
- **Management is a skill**, and improves with deliberate attention.

## Where it shows in Classroom Manager

Computers add questions of their own: when screens are down, what students may open, how work is handed in. Clear rules and procedures help, and software can support them. Classroom Manager does not view or record student screens, so it supports the rules a teacher sets without turning into surveillance.

## Who should read it

New and experienced teachers, and school leaders who set behaviour policy.
