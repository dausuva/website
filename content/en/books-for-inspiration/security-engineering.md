**The argument.** Security is about people, incentives and failure as much as about cryptography. Anderson, a professor at Cambridge, surveys real systems, from banks and locks to phones and medical devices, and shows how they were attacked and why. The lesson is to design for the attacker you will actually meet and for the honest user who makes mistakes.

## Ideas worth keeping

- **Security is an economic and human problem** as well as a technical one.
- **Policy first.** Decide what must be protected from whom before choosing mechanisms.
- **Usability is security.** Systems people cannot use correctly will be used incorrectly.
- **Learn from failures.** Real incidents teach more than theory.
- **Protocols fail at the seams** between components and between organisations.

## Where it shows in Classroom Manager

Classroom Manager does not try to be a lock that cannot be picked. It is a classroom tool whose limits are stated honestly, for example that Exam mode is a classroom restriction and not a certified exam environment.

## Who should read it

Anyone who has to judge how safe a system is, including school leaders who sign off on software.
