Classroom Manager started in a school computer lab, where one person teaches informatics and also keeps twenty Windows PCs working. This page sets out what we found about how schools run their labs, what that costs, and where a flat-price tool pays for itself. **Figures marked "estimate" are our own working assumptions, not published statistics**; replace them with your school's numbers.

## How schools run a Windows lab today

| Approach | What it costs | What it leaves undone |
|---|---|---|
| **By hand**, walking from desk to desk | Nothing to buy; the teacher's or IT person's time | Every install, update and setting repeated on every PC; lessons start late |
| **Remote desktop**, one PC at a time | Free or low cost | Still one computer at a time; nothing for the whole room |
| **Free classroom tool** (Veyon) | Free; paid add-ons for website blocking, devices and discovery | Screen-focused; little lab administration; community support |
| **Commercial classroom suite** | Usually priced per device, per year | Often screen monitoring first; cost grows with every PC |
| **Cloud device management** (Intune, school MDM) | Licences per user or device; IT skills; internet for every action | Built for IT departments, not for the teacher in the room |
| **Classroom Manager** | €499 the first year per school, then €299 a year | No student-screen features by design; Windows only |

## Who it is for

- **Schools where a teacher is also the lab administrator.** In many schools informatics teachers or a part-time technician look after the labs alongside their other work (estimate: the most common case in Lithuanian general-education schools).
- **Gymnasiums and progymnasiums with one to three Windows labs** of 15 to 30 PCs each (estimate).
- **Vocational and private schools** that manage their own PCs without a central IT department.
- **Schools that will not record minors' screens** and want lab control that is easy to explain under GDPR.

Lithuania has about a thousand general-education schools (estimate from national education statistics), most with at least one computer lab, plus vocational schools and colleges.

## The time a lab takes

An example for one lab of 20 PCs. These are estimates; time your own lab for a week and put your numbers in.

| Task | By hand | With Classroom Manager | Saved per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lesson start: open the website or program, hand out files (3 lab lessons a day, 180 days) | 6 min | 1 min | 45 h |
| Collect the class's work (once a week, 36 weeks) | 15 min | 2 min | 8 h |
| Install or update a program (6 times a year) | 100 min | 15 min | 9 h |
| Check Windows updates and clocks (monthly) | 60 min | 5 min | 11 h |
| Reset wallpapers, layouts and settings students changed (weekly) | 20 min | 3 min | 10 h |
| **Total** | | | **about 83 hours** |

At an estimated €12 an hour of teacher or technician time, that is close to **€1,000 a year for one lab**, against €299 a year for support renewal. With two labs the time saved roughly doubles; the price stays the same.

## What it costs per computer

The price is per school, not per PC (one licence covers fewer than 50 computers; larger schools, ask us).

| Computers | First year | Each following year |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | €33 per PC | €20 per PC |
| 20 | €25 per PC | €15 per PC |
| 30 | €17 per PC | €10 per PC |
| 45 | €11 per PC | €7 per PC |

The first year includes the signed installation package, setup and rollout help, onboarding, and a year of updates and email and phone support. Renewal keeps the updates and support coming. **If a school does not renew, its installation keeps working**; new installs and updates then show Windows' unknown-publisher prompt until it does.

## Why a flat price

Schools budget per lab and per year, not per seat. A flat price keeps the cost the same when a lab grows by five PCs, needs no counting of devices, and makes the yearly decision simple: renew if the updates and help were worth €299.
