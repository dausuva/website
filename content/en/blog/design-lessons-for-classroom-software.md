A teacher uses a classroom tool with thirty students watching. There is no time to read a manual, and a wrong click is public. That is the situation we design for, and one book describes it better than any other: [The Design of Everyday Things](../../books-for-inspiration/design-of-everyday-things/).

## When people err, look at the design

If a person pulls a door that should be pushed, the handle is wrong, not the person. We apply the same test to every button in Classroom Manager: if a teacher picks the wrong one, we change the button.

## Say what happened

Every action should visibly do something, quickly. Silence feels like failure. That is why the Host shows what each computer did after a command, and why the status line reports the result.

## Make the dangerous step deliberate

Some actions should be hard to do by accident. Starting Exam mode asks for confirmation, and rotating a lab key asks you to type its name. A guard also refuses actions that would lock a teacher out of their own computer.

## Let people undo

A display change that nobody confirms goes back by itself after 15 seconds. Appearance changes can be reset exactly. Failed computers can be retried in one step.

None of this is new. It is good design applied to a setting where mistakes are expensive. If you want the original ideas, start with the book above.
